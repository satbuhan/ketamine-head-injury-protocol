# Ketamine in Head Injury — Safety Analysis & Complication Management — Interactive Decision Algorithm

An interactive, referenced walkthrough of the decision-making algorithm for
using ketamine in head injury — as an induction/RSI agent, as an ICU
sedation adjunct, or as an analgesic in a conscious patient — and for
managing the complications (hypotension, emergence reactions, seizures, a
rise in ICP, laryngospasm) that can follow. Every box and decision point in
the diagram can be selected to reveal, in one place: what the step means,
why the algorithm calls for it, the exact dosing as authored, and the
sources behind it.

**Live site:** https://satbuhan.github.io/ketamine-head-injury-protocol/

**Authors:** Dr. Özgür Karcıoğlu & Dr. Satuk Buğra Han Bozatlı

## Why this exists

A flowchart alone tells a reader *what* to do at each step but not *why*,
and a reader outside the authors' own team cannot easily check the evidence
behind a specific number (a dose cut-off, a shock-index threshold, a
hypotension rate). This page keeps the original algorithm exactly as
authored, and attaches the clinical reasoning and literature to each box
without cluttering the diagram itself.

The algorithm this page presents is Figure 2 of a systematic review by the
same authors, "Use of Ketamine in Head Injury: Safety Analysis and
Complication Management" (manuscript in preparation, 98 references); the
supplementary content on this page draws on that review's evidence base,
cross-checked against independent sourcing for every citation.

## Status

This is a working version, built to be tested on both desktop and mobile.
Several things are worth flagging explicitly:

- **The three indication pathways (A/B/C) are each two boxes, not one.**
  The source figure draws a header box ("A. Induction / RSI", etc.) with
  its own arrow down to a separate content box carrying the actual dosing
  and logic — not a single box with an internal label/sub split. This tool
  preserves that as six nodes (`branch-a-header`/`branch-a-content`, and
  likewise for B and C) rather than three, so the real arrow between each
  header and its content is a real edge in `assets/app.js`.
- **The complication-management decision draws only two arrows in the
  source**, not one per complication. "Complication recognised → manage"
  connects only to the Hypotension and ICP-rise boxes; the other four
  complication boxes (Emergence, Seizure, Laryngospasm, Reassess) are
  simply grouped beside them with no arrow of their own, confirmed against
  a high-resolution crop of the source figure. `assets/app.js`'s `EDGES`
  reflects exactly those two arrows, and the six complication boxes are
  laid out as the source draws them — two stacked columns of three
  (`.flow-wrap-comp` in `assets/style.css`), not one row of six.
- **Reference 51 (Ramadurai et al., 2026) could not be independently
  located.** Exhaustive searching — exact title, author combination,
  DOI/Crossref lookup, and the full reference list of a contemporaneous
  (2026) ketamine-in-TBI review that would plausibly cite it if real —
  found no trace of this paper. It has been removed from the `refs` list
  of the ICU-sedation content box (`branch-b-content`) that cited it; that
  box's other supporting reference (Burman et al. 2025) already covers its
  claims. The entry remains in the full bibliography with this finding
  recorded in its own `kind` field, rather than silently dropped, and
  should be raised with the manuscript authors.
- **Reference 16 (Carlson et al. 2018) is corrected.** The manuscript
  characterised its cohort as "10 patients with severe brain injury"; the
  actual trial enrolled 10 patients with acute brain injury, of whom only
  2 had severe TBI (the other 8 had aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage).
  The dose-dependent spreading-depolarisation-suppression finding itself is
  accurately represented. Corrected in `kind`.
- **Reference 88 (Avidan et al. 2017) has a corrected title.** The
  manuscript gave "...for prevention of..."; the paper's real title is
  "...does not affect...", which is in fact consistent with its null
  result for delirium. Corrected in both `text` and `kind`.
- **Reference 63 was fabricated-as-cited and has been corrected.** The
  manuscript cited "Daghmouri, Chaouch, Noomen" in the *European Journal of
  Emergency Medicine* for the etomidate-vs-ketamine RSI comparison; that
  DOI resolves to an unrelated 2017 paper, and no paper by those authors on
  this topic could be found anywhere despite exhaustive searching. The
  underlying claim is real, though: the actual systematic review/
  meta-analysis covering this exact comparison was located and substituted
  in its place — Greer et al. (2025), *Critical Care Medicine*. **This is
  the one fabrication finding that matters for what's on screen**: ref 63
  is actively cited by the induction/RSI content box (`branch-a-content`),
  so this correction changes a reference the reader actually sees, not
  just the bibliography.
- **Reference 79 is confirmed fabricated** — exhaustive multi-angle
  searching (title, authors, DOI, journal/volume/article number) found no
  trace of it anywhere. It fits the same pattern as the confirmed-fabricated
  ref 51 above (a generic review title, a very recent year, an unusually
  specific article number). It is not used on any flowchart node and has
  not been replaced, since no real paper was found to stand in for it;
  flagged in its own `kind` field and should be raised with the manuscript
  authors.
- **Reference 52 remains unverifiable** — no trace found, but also no
  fabrication signal (unlike 51/79). Not used on any flowchart node;
  flagged rather than treated as confirmed.
- **References 56 and 76 had minor year corrections** (epub-ahead-of-print
  vs. final print year: 2023→2024 and 2019→2020 respectively), confirmed
  against the publisher's own record.
- **The remaining references — including the entire evidence basis for the
  laryngospasm box (refs 92–95) and the specific 81%-seizure-response claim
  behind the seizure box (ref 80) — have now been independently verified**
  against primary sources, closing out what an earlier pass had to leave
  as "pending" when this session's shared web-search budget ran out
  mid-task.
- **Four references (42–45), the canonical GRADE methodology papers, still
  carry a "not independently re-searched live this session" caveat** in
  their own `kind` field. Baseline confidence in these is very high — they
  are foundational, widely-cited methodology papers — but they were not
  re-checked against a live primary source this pass, and that's stated
  plainly rather than presented as equivalent to the verification done on
  everything else.

Every reference above not flagged as fabricated or unverifiable has been
independently re-verified against its primary source rather than accepted
on trust from the manuscript's own list; anything not yet checked, or found
to be wrong, is marked as such in its own `kind` field rather than
presented as confirmed. Everything else not flagged above is paraphrased
from a cited source, listed in the References section at the bottom of the
page.

This tool also introduces the atlas's first non-single-row complication
group: `assets/style.css`'s `.flow-wrap-comp` lays out six boxes as two
columns of three (`grid-auto-flow: column`) rather than the generic
single-row `flow-wrap-N` classes every other tool uses, matching how the
source figure actually draws its two stacked complication columns.

## Disclaimer

This is an academic / educational reference tool, not a substitute for
institutional protocol or bedside clinical judgement. Verify any dose or
threshold against current local protocol before clinical use.

## How it's built

Plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step and no framework — a single static site
that GitHub Pages can serve directly.

```
index.html          page structure
assets/style.css     design (the "paper" masthead/references,
                      the diagram scope + readout panel)
assets/content.js     ← single source of truth for every node's text,
                      dosing, and reference list. Edit this file to change
                      what the page says.
assets/app.js         rendering + interaction (builds the diagram and the
                      connector lines from content.js, handles the
                      click-to-expand panel on desktop and the bottom
                      sheet on mobile)
```

### Running it locally

No install needed:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000
```

### Editing content

Open `assets/content.js`. Each entry in `NODES` is one box or decision
diamond from the algorithm (`meaning`, `rationale`, optional `dose` table,
and `refs` pointing at numbered entries in `REFERENCES`). Add or edit a
reference in `REFERENCES` and point to it by `id` from any node. Never
change a node's `label` or `sub` text without the author team's
sign-off — those fields are the algorithm itself, transcribed verbatim from
Figure 2. Boxes with a YES/NO split or an itemized list (e.g.
`branch-a-content`'s shock-index-gated doses) carry the source box's own
line breaks as literal `\n` — preserve those exactly; they render as
separate lines, not flattened into comma-separated prose, because that is
how the original boxes are drawn.
